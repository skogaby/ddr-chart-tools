# Prototype Results: Offset Estimation on Stock DDR World

Throwaway prototype: `prototypes/sync-probe/` (Rust, links this crate's SSQ
parser and XWB decoder; `analyze*.py` for statistics). Run 2026-09-28 against
`$DDR_WORLD_INSTALL/data/` and the modpack's `judgement_offsets.csv`.

```
cd .agents/planning/2026-09-28-auto-sync/prototypes/sync-probe
cargo build --release
./target/release/sync-probe "$DDR_WORLD_INSTALL/data" <path/to/judgement_offsets.csv> out/full
python3 analyze2.py out/full                  # feature / template / rule comparison
python3 analyze3.py out/full rise1k notes     # headline stats, gating, shifted sources
```

## Setup

- **Corpus:** 1,434 of the 1,440 songs with SSQ + XWB + community value.
  Excluded by pre-existing tool limits, not by the prototype:
  - `lich`, `ifly`: `ssq::parse` rejects a freeze-end with no matching head.
  - `hart`, `itiz`, `mixn`, `souv`: XWB lacks `WBND` magic.
- **Measured quantity:** `m` = audio onset time − chart event time, in ms.
  For each integer shift `d`, the score is `Σ wᵢ · env(tᵢ + d)`, where `tᵢ` is
  each event's time from the SSQ tempo pairs and `env` is the onset envelope.
  `m` is the argmax, refined parabolically.
- **Ground truth:** the community value `c`. The maintainer reports it was
  generated by an automated tool ("FinalOffset" or similar) and has been
  validated in play by the community. Agreement below is therefore agreement
  with a play-validated automated method, not with direct perception.
- **Synthetic large errors:** each stock chart is re-scored as if its sync had
  been moved by ±50 … ±200 ms. A correct estimator recovers the same truth
  regardless of the shift.

## Findings

### 1. Sign: `m = T − c` — the opposite of the reading in `calibration-corpus.md`

| Fit | Robust std. dev. (1,434 songs) |
|-----|------|
| `m − c` | 11.06 ms |
| `m + c` | **0.72 ms** |

A song with `c > 0` has its music landing `c` ms *earlier* than a well-synced
song, relative to the arrows. Fixing it in the chart means moving the chart
`c` ms **earlier** (subtracting `c` from every `tempo_data`). The modpack
doc's "positive = judged later" must mean the player's input is treated as
later, which is the correct compensation for early music. The data are
unambiguous; the target formula becomes `T = median(m + c)`.

### 2. Agreement with the community values is at the quantization floor

Best configuration (`rise1k` feature, note template, ±200 ms window, no
gating):

- `T = −1.74 ms`. The 107 songs with `c = 0` give `median(m) = −1.54 ms`,
  consistent.
- Residual `m + c − T`: robust std. dev. **0.72 ms**; 78.9% within 1 ms, 90.2%
  within 2 ms. The community values are integers, so about 0.3 ms of this is
  rounding.
- For comparison, stock charts as shipped are within 1 ms / 2 ms / 5 ms of
  correct for 20% / 35% / 63% of songs.

When the estimator locks onto the right peak, it and the community's tool
agree almost exactly.

### 3. The dominant failure is half-beat aliasing, and it sits inside ±200 ms

With the ±200 ms window, 6.1% of stock songs were mis-estimated. Of 87
failures, 35 were off by exactly ±0.50 beat (e.g. `dead` 190 BPM → −160 ms,
`brak` 162 BPM → −186 ms, `lali` 170 BPM → +177 ms). The best competing peak
in those songs scores 0.94–1.00 of the true peak.

The cause is the music, not the template. DDR's catalogue is dominated by
150–200 BPM tracks with strong off-beat onsets (hi-hats, off-beat bass), and
charts are dense in eighth notes. Half a beat at those tempos is 150–200 ms,
which is exactly the proposed window.

**A chart that is off by half a beat is indistinguishable from an in-sync
chart to any onset-based method on this music.** That is a physical limit, not
a tuning problem. Smaller aliases also occur: quarter-beat errors on some
98–140 BPM songs, e.g. `rain` 140 BPM → −105 ms.

The remaining failures are small (5–20 ms) disagreements with the community
value, and a few songs where one of the two methods is plausibly wrong
(e.g. `puty`: community −11, measured −1.8, sharp unambiguous peak).

### 4. Template and feature comparison (D6, D15)

Percentage of songs within 5 ms of truth, ±200 ms window, highest peak taken
(no gating). "Shift" is the synthetic sync error applied to the source.

| Feature | Template | Stock | ±50 | ±100 | ±150 |
|---------|----------|-------|-----|------|------|
| `flux` (1024-pt, full band) | notes | 92 | 89–90 | 89 | 87 |
| `rise` (10 ms window, emphasis peak 3 kHz) | notes | 94 | 91–92 | 90–91 | 87–88 |
| **`rise1k`** (10 ms window, emphasis peak 1 kHz) | **notes** | **94** | **93–94** | **93** | **90** |
| `rise1k` | beat grid | 90 | 87–88 | 84–86 | 72–77 |
| `rise1k` | metrical-weighted notes | 93 | 92–93 | 91 | 87–88 |
| `riselp` (30–500 Hz only) | notes | 73 | 72–73 | 72 | 68–70 |
| `superflux`-style (205-song sample only) | notes | 85 | — | — | — |

- The note template beats the beat grid everywhere, and the gap widens with
  the error size. **D6 holds**, but its rationale was too strong: the note
  pattern reduces aliasing rather than eliminating it.
- The short-window rising-edge feature (the +9ms or Null? approach) beats
  plain and SuperFlux-style spectral flux. Moving its emphasis from 3 kHz to
  1 kHz helps a little.
- Low-band-only features, combinations of features, and letting a low-band
  curve break near-ties did not beat `rise1k` alone.

### 5. Confidence gating makes the ±200 ms window safe (D8, D9, D17)

Gate: refuse when the strongest rival peak (more than 25 ms away) scores
≥ 0.9 of the best, when the two halves of the song disagree by more than
10 ms, or when the peak is on the window edge.

`rise1k` / notes, with gating. "Correct" means within 5 ms of the community
truth; a refused song keeps its source sync unchanged.

| Source error | ±200 ms: correct / wrong / refused | Tempo-scaled window (±0.45 beat): correct / wrong / refused | Hybrid: correct / wrong / refused |
|---|---|---|---|
| stock | 85.0 / 2.1 / 12.9 | 94.6 / 2.4 / 3.1 | 94.6 / 2.4 / 3.1 |
| ±50 ms | 82–85 / 3 / 13–16 | 82–84 / 3 / 13–15 | 83–86 / 3 / 11–14 |
| ±100 ms | 78–80 / 3 / 17–19 | 75–77 / 8 / 15–18 | 81–84 / 4 / 12–15 |
| ±150 ms | 63–65 / 3 / 32–34 | 47–48 / 26–28 / 25–26 | 66 / 16–17 / 17–19 |

The hybrid runs the ±200 ms pass first and falls back to the narrow window
near the source sync when that pass refuses.

- **±200 ms with gating** is wrong about 3% of the time at *every* error size.
  It refuses more, but a refusal leaves the chart as it was. Refusals on
  already-synced songs cost nothing.
- **The tempo-scaled window** is best on small errors but gives
  *confidently wrong* answers once the true error exceeds about 0.45 beat:
  the aliased peak is inside the window and looks legitimate.
- **The hybrid** is in between. It picks up the tempo-scaled window's
  small-error wins but inherits its large-error failures (16% wrong at
  ±150 ms).
- Effect on the stock catalogue, ±200 ms with gating: songs within 5 ms of
  correct go from 63.2% to 92.9%. 21 of 1,434 end up more than 2 ms worse than
  stock; 4 of those (0.3%) are left more than 20 ms off.
- Split-half disagreement correlates with error; the 10 ms threshold (D17)
  removes about a quarter of the remaining wrong answers (39 → 30 songs).

### 6. Calibration target

`T = −1.74 ms` for `rise1k` / notes. Each feature has its own `T` (`flux`
−4.4, `rise` −2.1, `riselp` −0.9), confirming the constant absorbs detector
bias. `T` must be re-derived whenever the feature or its parameters change.
The design should keep the calibration run reproducible.

### 7. Cost

Median per song on 12 contended threads:

| Stage | Time |
|-------|------|
| MS-ADPCM decode | 59 ms |
| One `rise`-family envelope | 315 ms (about 150 ms single-threaded, uncontended) |
| All score curves | 30 ms |

Songs run 112 s at the median and 151 s at most, so the single-threaded
production cost is well under the 2 s budget (D19). A debug build will be much
slower (`rustfft` without optimization), so unit tests should use short
synthetic signals.

## 8. Narrow cap (maintainer override, 2026-09-28): ±60 ms

The maintainer judged ±200 ms too aggressive and asked for a cap of 55–60 ms,
wide enough for the legacy console (Ultramix) offset. Re-analysed from the
same score curves (`analyze4.py`, `analyze5.py`).

### Aliasing disappears; the best feature changes

With the search at ±55 … ±80 ms, results are identical to two decimals: no
half-beat alias fits inside (the nearest quarter-beat alias needs ≥ 214 BPM,
and the ≥ 220 BPM subset shows no degradation). Without aliasing, raw
precision decides, and the 3 kHz-emphasis `rise` feature beats `rise1k`.

Residual `e` = (our correction) − (community correction), all 1,434 songs, no
gating:

| Feature / template, ±60 ms | Robust std. dev. | Within 1 ms | Within 2 ms | Within 3 ms | P99 | Same whole-ms value | Within ±1 whole ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`rise` / notes** | **0.47 ms** | **91.1%** | **97.2%** | **98.0%** | 5.7 ms | 69.4% | 95.5% |
| `rise` / metrical notes | 0.47 | 92.3 | 97.3 | 98.2 | 5.8 | 72.0 | 95.6 |
| `rise` / beat grid | 0.47 | 93.2 | 96.9 | 98.0 | 6.2 | 73.9 | 95.7 |
| `rise1k` / notes | 0.69 | 80.8 | 92.7 | 94.8 | 11.6 | 53.3 | 89.4 |
| `flux` / notes | 0.73 | 79.1 | 92.8 | 96.4 | 8.3 | 50.8 | 88.8 |
| mean of `rise` + `rise1k` + `flux` | 0.57 | 85.9 | 94.5 | 96.7 | 7.4 | 61.8 | 92.2 |

For comparison, stock charts as shipped are within 1 / 2 / 3 ms of correct for
20.1% / 34.9% / 43.8% of songs. `T = −2.10 ms` for `rise` / notes.

- Templates are indistinguishable once aliasing is gone. The note template
  keeps the smallest P99.
- Ensembles do not help: the other features are noisier, not independently
  noisy.
- The residual shows no dependence on tempo, note count, or the size of the
  community correction (`e = 0.02·c + 0.05`).

### Where the floor is

The community values are whole milliseconds, so rounding alone contributes
0.29 ms std. dev. to the residual. The remaining continuous disagreement is
about √(0.47² − 0.29²) ≈ 0.37 ms.

- "Same whole-ms value" cannot approach 100% even with perfect agreement:
  when the true value sits near x.5 ms, the two tools round differently. With
  0.37 ms of disagreement, about 70–75% exact integer agreement is expected —
  which is what we see.
- Against a whole-ms reference, nothing below ±1 ms is verifiable. For the
  363–393 songs whose community correction is only 1–2 ms, we pick the same
  whole-ms value 77–78% of the time, the same direction 97–98%, and are
  within 1 ms 98–99%.

### Gating in the narrow regime

The rival-peak gate fires on only 2 songs within ±70 ms, `rint` and `help`
(see §9). Both are genuinely bistable: two alignments 22–37 ms apart score
within 7% of each other. Split-half disagreement is the useful signal
otherwise:

| Split-half gate | Accepted | Of accepted: within 1 ms | within 2 ms | > 3 ms off | Whole catalogue within 2 ms after auto-sync |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 99.9% | 91.1% | 97.2% | 29 songs | 97.1% |
| ≤ 5 ms | 95.6% | 91.8% | 97.7% | 21 songs | 94.4% |
| ≤ 3 ms | 89.1% | 93.3% | 98.1% | 15 songs | 90.3% |

Refused songs keep their stock error, which is why stricter gating lowers
the whole-catalogue figure even though accepted answers get tighter.

### Search margin beyond the cap

With the search window equal to the cap (±60), a true correction of 55 ms
sits 5 ms from the edge: 25–28% of those are refused and 13% of the accepted
are more than 5 ms off. Searching ±70 ms and then refusing any result beyond
the ±60 ms cap fixes this. At ±55 ms synthetic error: 91–94% accepted, 97%
of them within 2 ms. Precision elsewhere is unchanged.

### Largest disagreements (confident on our side)

These are songs where our estimate has a sharp single peak and both halves
agree within 1 ms, yet differ from the community value by 7 ms or more. The
two tools cannot say which is right; only listening can.

Corrections are "move the chart this many ms later"; negative means earlier.

## 9. Comparison with the community pipeline (`ssq2sm.py`, `ghoststep-ssc`)

The maintainer was sent the tooling now used to produce the community
offsets:

- `ssq2sm.py`: a stdlib-only SSQ → SM/SSC converter.
- `ghoststep-ssc`: 1,507 SSCs it produced from the stock catalogue, plus
  `_manifest.json`.

Neither is in this repository. The offset-measuring tool itself was not
included.

### What the converted SSCs contain

Every `ghoststep-ssc` file's `#OFFSET` equals the stock SSQ's own sync. For
1,439 of the 1,440 matched songs the difference is exactly 0; the SSCs carry
no corrections. They are the stock charts in StepMania form, presumably the
input to the offset tool. The community values were integers when they
reached the modpack: its generator parses `int(fields[1])`. So no sub-ms
ground truth is available from what was shared. Any sub-ms values would have
to come from the offset tool's raw output.

### Sign convention

`ssq2sm.py` writes the StepMania convention, `#OFFSET = −anchor₀ / 1000`,
the same convention this tool's SSC writer uses. The modpack generator
negates the community tool's value on the way into the CSV ("sync error →
compensating offset"). Combined with §1:

- The community tool's raw value `r = −c` is "how late the music is relative
  to the chart", i.e. "move the chart `r` ms later".
- That is the same sign and meaning as this prototype's correction.
- The CSV value `c` is "ms to add to `#OFFSET`" (equivalently, subtract from
  `tempo_data`).

This matches the maintainer's observation that the scripts and the CSV have
opposite signs.

### Timing model: "True to DDR" vs raw SSQ timing

`ssq2sm.py` documents the game's anchor normalization as confirmed from the
executable (`SsqReader::prepare()`):

```
numerator = int32(v * 1000); x = f32(numerator); x = f32(x / f32(tps));
x = f32(x + 0.5); anchor_ms = floor(x)
```

Every tempo anchor is rounded to whole ms before BPMs are derived. It calls
the exact `v / tps` timing "True to SSQ" and says it is not what the game
plays ("up to ~0.84 ms off per arrow"). This matters only for files whose
TPS is not 1000. In the analysed stock set that is **723 of 1,434 songs
(TPS 150)**; the other 711 are TPS 1000, where both models are identical.

**Verified in Ghidra (2026-09-28).** The normalization is in
`step::SsqReader`'s prepare routine, found through the class's RTTI and
vtable. It is identical in `gamemdx_20250805` (`FUN_1801b6100`),
`gamemdx_20260825` (`FUN_1801cc040`) and `gamemdx_20260915`
(`FUN_1801cbb20`):

```
MOV   ECX, tempo_data[i]
IMUL  ECX, ECX, 0x3e8        ; int32 multiply by 1000 (wraps)
CVTDQ2PS XMM0, ECX           ; -> float
CVTDQ2PS XMM1, tps           ; tempo chunk param2 (u16) -> float
DIVSS XMM0, XMM1
ADDSS XMM0, [0.5f]
CALL  floorf
CVTTSS2SI ECX, XMM0          ; -> int32 ms, stored per anchor
```

The note-list builder in `gamemdx_20260825` (`FUN_1801cc280`) seeds each
TIMING note's `musicCount` (an int32, initialized to `INT32_MIN`) from that
array. So the game's clock is whole milliseconds at every tempo anchor.
`ssq2sm.py`'s description is exact. Not checked: how non-TIMING notes are
interpolated between anchors. Their `musicCount` is also int32, so each note
is quantized to 1 ms too, and any rounding-mode bias there is constant and
absorbed by `T`.

The prototype was re-run with that normalization (`--timing ddr`, `rise`
feature only, `analyze6.py`). Residual against the community values, search
±70 ms, no gating:

| Timing | Subset | n | Robust std. dev. | Within 1 ms | Within 2 ms | Same whole-ms value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw (`v / tps`) | TPS 1000 | 711 | 0.444 ms | 94.0% | 97.6% | 72.9% |
| raw | TPS 150 | 723 | 0.515 | 88.2 | 96.8 | 66.0 |
| True to DDR | TPS 1000 | 711 | 0.444 | 94.0 | 97.6 | 73.0 |
| True to DDR | TPS 150 | 723 | 0.577 | 86.0 | 96.3 | 61.8 |

- **On TPS 1000 songs the two approaches are identical, and agreement is
  best: 94% within 1 ms.**
- On TPS 150 songs, raw timing agrees *better* with the community values.
  The likely explanation is that the values were measured against raw timing,
  e.g. with the "legacy" `--ssq-timing` mode that `ssq2sm.py` still keeps
  "to reproduce older output". The two timing models move our per-song
  estimate by a median of 0.00 ms, P90 0.33 ms: below the integer CSV's
  resolution.
- `rint` and `help` flip by 22–37 ms between the two runs. That is not a
  timing-model effect: they are the two bistable songs the rival gate
  refuses under either model.

**Conclusion.** Neither timing model is clearly better *against this
reference*, but the game plays "True to DDR", so that is the correct model.
The community values for TPS 150 songs appear to have been measured against
raw timing and carry up to about 0.3 ms (P90) of per-song error relative to
what the game plays. The disagreement they could explain is at most about 0.3 ms on
half the catalogue, which the integer CSV cannot resolve. If `ssq2sm.py`'s
reverse engineering is right, "True to DDR" is what players hear on TPS 150
charts. Beyond that, the one difference this makes to the auto-sync design is to
**calibrate `T` and measure accuracy on the 711 TPS 1000 stock songs**,
where the question does not arise. Every chart this tool writes for DDR is
TPS 1000 (business rule 3), so its output has no normalization ambiguity.

This tool's existing DDR → SM5 path uses raw `v / tps` timing for TPS 150
sources, which is now confirmed to be up to about 0.5 ms per anchor away from
what DDR plays (`ssq2sm.py` quotes up to ~0.84 ms per arrow). It is
independent of auto-sync and noted as a follow-up.

The game cannot express anything finer than 1 ms at an anchor, and the SSQs
this tool writes are TPS 1000 with whole-ms anchors. So ±0.5 ms is the floor
of what any DDR-output correction can mean in-game.

### The largest disagreements are not timing-model effects

`hane`, `reci`, `wwto`, `tiho` (§8) are all TPS 1000 songs.
